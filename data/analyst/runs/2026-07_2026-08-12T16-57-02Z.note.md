# Published note 2026-07 (run 2026-08-12T16:57:02Z)

Verdict: AUGMENTATION | 710 words (extended (a month with something to explain)) | 25 numbers
Counts are reported, not enforced: length is proportional to what changed and the number ceiling is retired.
Notification: No labour panel moved; augmentation reading holds on absence of harm, not proof of gain
Falsifier: The prime-age employment-share chart moves first — if its 12-month change falls to −0.6 points or lower by 10 November while control-industry job growth is still at or above 0.8 percent, the exposed outflow is no longer landing anywhere and this reading was wrong.

---

The reading remains augmentation, and it rests on the absence of deterioration rather than on any visible sign that AI is making workers more valuable: displacement has not removed people from the labour market, which is a narrower claim than augmentation working. Confidence is low.

Almost nothing moved this month. Every labour series on the dashboard is unchanged. The only two headline numbers that shifted were the gap between postings for knowledge work and postings for hands-on work, which widened three tenths of a point in a month on a series that routinely swings several points, and one AI model's rating against other models. Neither is news about employment.

The strongest support is that people are not being shed. The share of 25-to-54-year-olds with a job is 80.4 percent, unchanged over twelve months and slightly above its 2019 level. Layoffs, at 1.1 percent of employment, have fallen over the past year. Hiring is running above quitting and that margin widened. These are economy-wide measures, and they are the only ones that can answer whether an outflow from one industry landed somewhere else, because no survey here follows a person from a shrinking industry into a growing one. In aggregate, nobody went missing.

Read the hiring and layoff figures together, though, and they describe a frozen market, not a healthy one. Hiring at 3.4 percent and quitting at 2.0 percent are both low by pre-pandemic standards while layoffs sit at or below normal. Employers are neither pushing people out nor taking anyone on. A freeze protects incumbents and does its damage to people trying to enter — and the employment measure above is dominated by people who already have jobs.

Against the reading: employment in AI-exposed industries — information, professional and business services, finance — is shrinking outright, down 1.19 percent over the year, while industries AI has barely touched are growing 1.16 percent. The exposed side is contracting in absolute terms. Rates are the obvious alternative explanation, but construction is the most interest-rate-sensitive industry in the economy and it sits on the growing side, so this is not a general borrowing-cost story.

The one positive strand is pay, and it has a catch. In AI-exposed industries pay is rising 4.22 percent a year against 3.21 percent in un-exposed ones — backwards from what replacement would produce. But employment in those same exposed industries is falling, and when entry-level hiring stops, the remaining workforce skews senior and the average rises without anyone getting a raise. A frozen market is exactly when that distortion is largest. Tracking the same individuals, job-switchers are getting 4.4 percent against 3.6 percent for stayers, which says movers land well at the median — but that covers the whole economy and cannot be cut to exposed industries. The composition question is unresolved, and that is why confidence is low.

Three further things sit badly. The gap between broad underemployment and headline unemployment has widened to 3.8 points, up 0.2 over the year and wide by 2010s standards: that is people landing badly rather than not landing, and it is the most displacement-shaped number here. The prime-age employment share, flat over twelve months, is down 0.3 points over the last three — a sharp move for that series, and twelve-month changes are the last place a recent turn shows up. And economy-wide productivity growth slowed to 2.2 percent from 2.9 percent; if AI were delivering the gains the augmentation story needs, this is where they would appear, and this quarter they went the wrong way.

Separately, labour's share of national income is 51.0 percent, its lowest in records back to 1947, down nearly a full point in a year against a long-run drift of about a tenth of a point annually — roughly seven times the secular pace, outside a recession, with unemployment at 4.1 percent. It is economy-wide, heavily revised, has been drifting down since the 1970s for reasons older than AI, and cannot be attributed to exposed work. It does not move the reading. It is the number that makes holding the reading least comfortable.

Watch the prime-age employment share — the share of 25-to-54-year-olds with a job. If that starts falling while un-exposed industries keep adding jobs, the exposed outflow is no longer landing anywhere.
