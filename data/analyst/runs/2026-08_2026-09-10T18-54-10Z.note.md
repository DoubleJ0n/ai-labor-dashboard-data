---

The displacement reading holds this month: AI-exposed industries are shedding jobs while the broader labor market has not stepped in to absorb the slack. This is based on measured cooling, not merely the absence of good news. Nothing else moved since last month's data; the only change anywhere on the dashboard was a small rise in the share of firms reporting AI use in any business function, to 23.2% from 22.4%, which shows AI deployment keeps spreading but says nothing about who it displaces.

The case for the verdict: jobs in AI-exposed industries (information, professional and business services, finance) fell 1.47% over the past year while jobs in industries AI barely touches (construction, leisure and hospitality, education and health) rose 1.4%, a gap that has widened for four straight months. Separately, and covering the whole economy rather than just exposed industries, the share of 25- to 54-year-olds holding a job slipped 0.3 percentage point over the past year, real deterioration, though the level itself remains slightly above its pre-pandemic mark, so this is a cooling, not a collapse. Three ordinary explanations, interest-rate sensitivity, the old 2023 media strikes, and staffing-sector cyclicality, were checked against the data and none fits: the control group includes construction, one of the most rate-sensitive industries there is, and it is still growing, which argues against a pure-rate story.

The strongest thing against the verdict is the shape of the exit. Layoffs are low and still falling, not rising, and hiring is neither speeding up nor slowing abnormally, the pattern of a hiring freeze rather than active firing, a meaningfully weaker claim than mass displacement. More importantly, job postings, the indicator that usually moves first, have been flat for six months: postings for knowledge-heavy work sit about 23% below pre-pandemic levels and postings for hands-on work about 5% above, but that gap has not widened further. If AI displacement were accelerating now, this is where it should show up first, and it has not.

What to watch: the postings gap between knowledge-work and hands-on-work industries, since that is the panel most likely to move first if this situation is turning either direction.
