**Overturn condition, and why these numbers.**

My verdict is AUGMENTATION, so the overturn is displacement appearing: the labour market ceasing to absorb the exposed outflow.

Primary: reabsorption `headline.change_over_12_months` at or below **−0.6 percentage points**. Why that number and not the panel's own −0.10 deterioration line: −0.10 is roughly flat and would fire on ordinary noise. More importantly, **this field printed −0.5 in 2026-06 and −0.5 in 2025-07**, in months I would read exactly as I am reading now. Per the worked example's discipline, a threshold that an already-observed non-displacement process reached discriminates nothing, so −0.5 is disqualified and I go one notch past the observed floor to −0.6.

Can it fire? The recent observation range on this field across 2025-2026 is roughly +0.3 to −0.5, and the standard deviation of 12-month changes is 0.51-0.52 against a 2010s mean of +0.42. So −0.6 sits about 2σ below the baseline mean and only **0.1 beyond a value the field printed two months ago** — well inside a single monthly print's ordinary variation for this series, which routinely moves 0.1-0.2 a month. It is also currently being pushed that way: the 3-month change is −0.3 and falling at 1.95σ. And it publishes: this is a monthly CPS series with three more releases (August, September, October data) before 2026-11-10, so the window cannot close without new observations — which is why I did not put the primary condition on a quarterly panel like productivity or labour share, where a 90-day window could pass with zero new data and return a meaningless "not fired."

Could something other than my verdict being wrong carry it there? Yes — a general recession would, so would an ordinary cyclical downturn, so would a labour-supply shock. That is what the second condition is for. Requiring **exposed_vs_control_jobs `control_value` at or above 0.8%** means construction, leisure/hospitality and education/health are still adding jobs at a decent clip while the aggregate prime-age employment share falls 0.6 points. A general downturn cannot produce that combination: if the whole economy were contracting, the control leg would go with it. Control is at 1.16% now, so 0.8% allows meaningful drift without collapse, and both legs of the condition publish monthly. If both hold, the aggregate is losing employment while the un-exposed industries are healthy, which means the losses are concentrated where AI exposure is and are not being caught anywhere — removal, not reallocation, and my reading was wrong.

Note what this condition deliberately does *not* do: it does not fire on the exposed leg getting worse on its own. Exposed employment falling further is fully consistent with reallocation and I have already read −1.19% that way. It fires only when the aggregate stops absorbing, which is the specific thing my verdict claims is happening.
