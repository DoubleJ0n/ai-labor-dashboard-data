The reading stays augmentation: AI's effect on jobs still looks like reshuffling rather than destruction. That call rests on the absence of deterioration in the numbers that would show destruction, not on any direct evidence that AI is making workers more productive - worth saying plainly, since it shapes how much weight this verdict can bear.

Nothing of substance moved since last month. The gap between job postings in AI-exposed office work and in hands-on control work barely shifted, from 30.4 points below its pre-pandemic level to 30.1 points below - essentially flat. The only other panel that moved was a measure of AI model capability in head-to-head ratings, which has no established link to any job or wage number here. Every panel that actually speaks to the jobs question held exactly where it sat a month ago.

The strongest evidence for the augmentation reading: prime-age employment (the share of 25-54 year olds working) has not moved at all over the past year, while hiring and quitting both rose over the same period and layoffs kept falling, with no widening in the number of people stuck in part-time work who want full-time. People leaving AI-exposed work are not stacking up unemployed or landing in worse jobs.

The strongest evidence against it, and it should not be softened: economy-wide productivity growth - a different, broader population than the exposed industries below - slowed to 2.2% over the past year, down from 2.9% the quarter before, its weakest pace in over a year. This is the number that would show AI gains paying off, and it moved the wrong way. Separately, employment specifically in AI-exposed industries kept falling, down 1.6% over the past year, while employment in industries AI barely touches grew 1.19% over the same period - and that gap has been parked in roughly the same place for months rather than closing, which is what a genuine reallocation story would predict.

What to watch: the reabsorption panel's prime-age employment ratio. If that starts falling while hiring in AI-exposed industries doesn't recover, this reading is wrong.
