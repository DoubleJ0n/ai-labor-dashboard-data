Almost nothing changed since last month. Eleven of the twelve charts behind this reading are unchanged. The one that moved: job postings for AI-exposed occupations (software, analysis, writing) widened their gap against postings for hands-on occupations (construction, hospitality, health and education work) for a sixth straight month. Exposed-occupation postings now sit 21.6% below their pre-pandemic level; hands-on-occupation postings sit 8.7% above theirs.

The strongest thing supporting the reading is the employment rate for prime-age workers, ages 25 to 54, which has not moved at all over the past year and sits three-quarters of a point above its already-strong 2019 level. If AI were pushing meaningful numbers of people out of work, this is the chart that would show it first, and it shows nothing. Employment in AI-exposed industries is down 1.35% over the past year while employment in industries AI barely touches is up a healthy 1.17% — a general downturn would hit both sides, and it isn't.

The strongest thing against the reading is that same job-postings gap. It is the most forward-looking chart on the dashboard, it leads the employment numbers by months, and it has now moved the wrong way for six consecutive months while everything else stayed calm. A second, smaller warning sign points the same direction: the share of unemployed workers out of work long-term is rising and that rise is accelerating, which looks less like people being actively fired and more like a labor market where anyone who exits a job, for any reason, is taking longer to land the next one.

What to watch: the job postings chart for exposed versus untouched occupations — it is the one panel currently moving against the calm reading everywhere else, and if the quiet employment numbers are going to turn, this is where it would show up first.
