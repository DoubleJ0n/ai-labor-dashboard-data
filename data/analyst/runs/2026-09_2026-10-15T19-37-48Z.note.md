---

The read on AI and jobs stays unchanged this month: augmentation, not displacement, best fits the data, and that call rests mainly on the absence of a reabsorption breakdown rather than on proof that AI is making workers more valuable where they sit.

Almost nothing moved. Of the tracked indicators, only two shifted, each slightly: the gap between job postings in AI-exposed industries and postings elsewhere widened a touch further, and the yield curve eased slightly while staying nowhere near inverted. Everything else is identical to last month.

The strongest support for augmentation is the prime-age (25 to 54) employment rate, which the data treat as the deciding measure for whether people who lose exposed-industry jobs are finding work elsewhere. It has not fallen over the past year and sits above its pre-pandemic level. Meanwhile employment in AI-exposed industries is still down about 1.6% over the past year while comparable, less-exposed industries are growing about 1.2% - a gap that has now held past its warning line for two straight months. Combined, the pattern reads as workers moving between jobs rather than being shut out of the labor market, since control industries are growing, not just shrinking less, which argues against a general economic slowdown as the cause.

The strongest evidence against the verdict is job postings. Postings in AI-exposed industries sit about 22% below their pre-pandemic level while postings in control industries sit about 9% above theirs, and that gap widened slightly again this month instead of narrowing. Postings usually move before actual employment does, so if augmentation were really taking hold, a recovery should show up here first. It hasn't. A second loose thread: the share of unemployed workers out of work long-term rose 3.5 points over the past year, moving the wrong way even though it hasn't yet crossed its own warning line.

The chart to watch is job postings for AI-exposed versus control industries: it tends to move first, and a turn there - up or down - would be the earliest sign this verdict is changing.
