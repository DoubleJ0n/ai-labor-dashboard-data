The reading stays displacement, and confidence in it stays low. Nothing in the underlying labor data moved since last month; the only figure that changed at all is the share of firms reporting any AI use, up slightly to 23.8% of firms, which says nothing directly about jobs.

The case for displacement: employment in AI-exposed industries has fallen 1.47% over the past year while similar non-exposed industries grew 1.4% — a gap that has been widening for four straight readings, not a one-month wobble.

The case against reading too much into that: the earliest-moving indicator here, job postings in AI-exposed occupations, has not worsened further in six months — it's essentially flat — even as the slower jobs and reabsorption data keep softening. If displacement were accelerating right now, the fastest-reacting series should show it first, and it doesn't. Separately, layoffs in the broader reabsorption data remain below normal and hiring-versus-quits is flat, not weak, meaning employers aren't actively cutting staff — new entrants are just struggling to get absorbed. That looks like a frozen hiring market, not a shedding one. A further complication: part of the exposed-industry weakness sits in temp staffing and rate-sensitive finance and real estate, sectors that soften early in any slowdown for reasons that have nothing to do with AI, and the data here can't rule that out.

What to watch: job postings for AI-exposed occupations, the fastest-moving series in this picture — if displacement is really taking hold, that's where it should show up next.
