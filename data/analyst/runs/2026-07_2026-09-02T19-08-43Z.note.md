The verdict is AUGMENTATION: nothing in the data shows AI displacing workers yet, though this reading rests on the absence of deterioration, not on proof that AI is making exposed workers better off.

Nothing material changed since last month. Job postings in AI-exposed occupations widened slightly against postings in hands-on occupations, and a capability benchmark ticked up further, but neither touched the two panels that actually decide this call.

The strongest support: the share of prime-age workers (25-54) who are employed has held flat over the past year and sits above its pre-pandemic level. Layoffs are low and falling, and hiring relative to quitting looks ordinary. That is not what a labor market shedding workers into unemployment looks like.

The strongest thing against it: underneath that flat 12-month figure, the same employment measure has been falling over just the last three months, at a pace that's starting to look unusual. If that continues, the 12-month figure will follow it down and this call flips. Separately, AI-exposed industries just crossed a line worth naming for the first time: employment there is shrinking year-over-year while employment in comparable non-exposed industries is growing normally, and this gap has held at roughly this level for four months without closing. And underemployment, people stuck in part-time work who want full-time hours, is close to a level this dashboard treats as a warning sign and has been rising over the year, which is exactly where displacement would show up first if it were arriving as reduced hours rather than layoffs.

One number that looks like good news but isn't clean: pay in AI-exposed industries is growing faster than in the rest of the economy, 4.2% against 3.2% over the past year. But employment in those same industries is shrinking, and shrinking industries mechanically pay survivors more regardless of whether AI is involved, so this can't be read as AI raising these workers' value.

What to watch: the three-month pace of the prime-age employment ratio, inside the reabsorption panel. It's already moving against this call, and if it keeps falling for another month or two, the flat 12-month figure it feeds will follow.
