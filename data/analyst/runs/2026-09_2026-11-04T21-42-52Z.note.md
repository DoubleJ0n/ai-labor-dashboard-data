Falsifier: This reading is overturned if the prime-age employment-population ratio's 12-month change (reabsorption headline) falls to -0.6 points or below while the exposed-minus-control jobs gap stays at or beyond -2.48, showing exposed-sector leavers are no longer landing elsewhere.

---

The reading this month is augmentation, but only in the thin sense that no destruction has shown up yet in the data — not that AI is visibly making exposed workers more productive or better paid. That distinction matters because the case rests entirely on an absence: nothing bad is clearly happening, rather than something good being clearly measured.

The real news this month has nothing to do with any number moving. No panel in this dashboard changed since the last read of this same September data, and that previous read, on the identical figures, called displacement. The flip rests on one measure that sits right on a dividing line: the prime-age (25 to 54) employment-population ratio was flat over the past twelve months, at 0.0 percent change. Last time, flat was read as recovery stalling out. This time, flat is read as damage not spreading further, especially since the level itself sits comfortably above its pre-pandemic benchmark. Nothing in the underlying economy changed between those two reads. The judgment about how to weigh a flat number did.

The strongest thing supporting the augmentation reading is that the industries outside the AI-exposed group are still healthy: jobs there are growing 1.19% over the past year, versus a 1.6% decline in AI-exposed industries like software-adjacent media and professional services. Layoffs are also falling, which points toward a frozen job market rather than one where employers are actively cutting staff. A weak national jobs report this month (only 29,000 payrolls added, unemployment at 4.2%) might suggest a broader slowdown, but that doesn't fit: a broad slowdown would hit the healthy control industries too, and it hasn't.

The strongest thing against it is that the gap between AI-exposed and other industries has been widening again through 2026 after narrowing in late 2025, and it's now at a level the data treats as historically unusual — not explained by the 2023 media strikes, which had already ended. Separately, and measured economy-wide rather than just in exposed industries, the share of unemployed workers who have been out of work long-term has risen 3.5 points over the past year, the closest any secondary reading on this dashboard comes to its own warning line. It hasn't crossed that line, but it's moving the wrong way, and it's exactly the kind of drift that would need to stay flat for an "exposed workers found other jobs" story to hold up cleanly. It isn't staying flat.

Two other figures looked informative but turned out not to be. Pay is growing faster in AI-exposed industries than elsewhere, which sounds like good news, but exposed employment is shrinking, and a shrinking group can show rising average pay just because its lower-paid members leave first, with no actual raise involved — so that number isn't being counted either way this month. And job postings, usually the earliest-moving signal here, are simply flat: postings for AI-exposed knowledge work remain about a fifth below pre-pandemic levels while postings for hands-on control work sit above theirs, and that gap has barely moved in six months. No new warning, but no recovery either, which is the main reason this call is held at low confidence rather than anything stronger.

Worth knowing plainly: this same verdict has swapped between displacement and augmentation repeatedly since July on data that barely moves month to month, and most of the conditions attached to those earlier calls were reported as triggered well before their deadlines. That could mean real lines were genuinely crossed each time, or it could mean the lines are easy to cross on ordinary noise. Either way, this month's augmentation call should be read as no more settled than last month's displacement call was.

What to watch: the prime-age employment-population ratio inside the reabsorption panel is the number that flipped this verdict once already with no other data changing, and it is the one to check first for any further move.
