The reading stays augmentation this month: no sign yet that AI is pushing employed workers out of their jobs faster than the economy can reabsorb them. That reading rests on the absence of bad news rather than on proof AI is making workers more productive, and nothing underneath it moved this month. The only two panels that shifted were workers' share of national income, which slipped slightly further on a decline that has been running for decades, and the interest-rate backdrop, which eased marginally. Neither is a panel this verdict depends on.

The strongest support is the prime-age employment-population ratio, the measure of whether people who lose jobs find new ones. It hasn't changed in the past year, and that flatness sits well within its normal historical range. Layoffs are running below their usual pace too. Together that's a labor market that has cooled its hiring but is not pushing people out of existing jobs in unusual numbers.

The strongest thing arguing the other way: job postings, broken out by occupation rather than industry, show postings for knowledge-type work continuing to lose ground against postings for hands-on work, a gap that has kept widening over the past six months on the freshest data available. Postings move before employment numbers do, and this one has moved the wrong way for six straight months without reversing. It has not yet shown up as actual job losses, but it is the live warning sign sitting underneath an otherwise calm headline, and it is the reason this call is held with low rather than higher confidence. Separately, AI-exposed industries are still hiring worse than non-exposed industries, with exposed employment shrinking while non-exposed employment grows, a gap that has now persisted for three months past the level treated as meaningful; interest-rate-sensitive construction, on the non-exposed side, is still growing fine, which argues against a simple broader-slowdown explanation.

Watch job postings by occupation next: it is the earliest-moving panel here, and it is the one currently moving against this reading.
