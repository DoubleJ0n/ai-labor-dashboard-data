The reading stays DISPLACEMENT this month, unchanged from last time, and still held at low confidence — this is not a case of augmentation working, nor a confirmed wave of job losses, but a specific weak spot in exposed industries that the rest of the labor market has not yet clearly resolved either way.

Nothing moved. Every series behind this call is showing the same month's data it showed last time, so there is no new development to report, only the same picture, worth restating because a reader coming to this fresh needs the whole case, not just what changed.

The strongest evidence for displacement: employment in AI-exposed industries fell 1.47% over the past year while employment in a comparison group of less-exposed industries (construction, hospitality, health care, education) grew 1.4%. That gap has widened for four straight months and this month crossed the level the dashboard treats as a clear warning. That is a real, building, industry-specific weakness.

The strongest evidence against it: most of what should accompany a genuine wave of displacement is missing. Economy-wide layoffs are falling, not rising. Hiring relative to quitting is flat rather than frozen. Long-term unemployment has only ticked up mildly. And the earliest-moving indicator here, the gap between postings for knowledge work and postings for hands-on work, actually narrowed slightly this month instead of widening further — the opposite of what a strengthening displacement story would need. On top of that, exposed-industry pay is rising faster than pay elsewhere, but a separate tracker that follows individual workers over time shows nothing unusual, suggesting the gap reflects junior workers leaving the shrinking industries rather than genuine pay acceleration. Three of the four signals that would normally confirm displacement are calm or improving, which is the reason this call carries low rather than medium confidence.

What to watch: the prime-age employment rate, the share of 25-to-54-year-olds working — if its year-over-year change turns clearly positive while jobs in the comparison industries keep growing, that would be the first sign this reading is wrong.
