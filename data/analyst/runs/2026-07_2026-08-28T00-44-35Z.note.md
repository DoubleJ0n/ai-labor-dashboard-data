The reading is augmentation, and nothing has moved since last month — this is the same call, on the same evidence, for a second month running.

That call rests on an absence of deterioration, not on any positive sign that AI is making workers more productive. The prime-age employment rate, which tracks whether adults are working at all regardless of industry, has been flat over the past year. Long-term unemployment is only mildly up, and hiring relative to quits is actually improving. That is the strongest thing behind the verdict: whatever is happening to exposed-industry jobs, the broader labor market is currently catching the fallout rather than failing to.

The strongest thing against it is that exposed-industry employment is actually shrinking — down 1.19% over the past year — while employment in industries AI barely touches, like construction and health care, is growing 1.16%. That gap has now sat in roughly the same place for four straight months without closing. On top of that, a broader measure of underemployment is edging up, and the reabsorption measure's own faster three-month reading is falling toward concerning territory well ahead of the slower twelve-month figure this whole call depends on. If that faster decline continues for another couple of months, the flat twelve-month reading behind this verdict could turn negative.

Two things looked like evidence but are not being counted as such. Pay is growing faster in exposed industries than in others, but exposed-industry employment is shrinking at the same time, so that faster pay growth could simply reflect fewer, more senior workers left in the average rather than anyone getting genuinely more valuable. And a broader productivity measure, where real augmentation should eventually show up economy-wide, actually eased this quarter rather than picking up.

What to watch: the reabsorption panel's headline prime-age employment measure, and specifically whether its faster three-month reading keeps sliding — that is the number that would move first if this reading is about to turn.
