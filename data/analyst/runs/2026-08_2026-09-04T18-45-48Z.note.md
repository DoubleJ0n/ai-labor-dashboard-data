# Published note 2026-08 (run 2026-09-04T18:45:48Z)

Verdict: DISPLACEMENT | 429 words (ordinary) | 16 numbers

Notification: Displacement returns: exposed-industry jobs falling, controls fine; confidence low.
Falsifier: This reading is wrong if the reabsorption panel's prime-age employment ratio (12-month change) climbs back to +0.2 or better within 90 days while the exposed-minus-control jobs gap is still at or below -2.0, which would show people landing again even as exposed industries stay specifically weak.

---

This month's reading is displacement, at low confidence. That call rests on real, specific deterioration in AI-exposed industries, not on the absence of good news elsewhere, but most of the signs you'd expect to see alongside actual displacement have not shown up yet.

What's new: the verdict flipped from augmentation last month to displacement this month, the second reversal in two months, which is itself a sign the underlying signal is close to a coin-flip right now rather than a settled trend. Driving the flip: the jobs gap between AI-exposed and control industries widened again, from -2.35 points in July to -2.87 in August, its third straight month of widening. Exposed industries shrank 1.47% over the year to August while control industries grew 1.4%, and since economy-wide numbers were unremarkable that same month (payrolls up 162,000, unemployment at 4.1%), this looks specific to AI-exposed work rather than a broad slowdown.

The strongest support for the verdict: the panel meant to show whether displaced workers are landing on their feet cracked for the first time this run. Prime-age (25-54) employment fell 0.3 percentage point over the year, having been roughly flat before. It's still 0.45 point above its pre-COVID level, so this is fraying at the edges, not collapse, but it's the first month this indicator has moved instead of holding steady.

The strongest thing against the verdict: job postings, which usually move ahead of actual jobs, are moving the other way. Postings for knowledge work still sit about 23% below pre-pandemic levels against about 5% above for hands-on work, but that gap has narrowed by roughly 3 points over the past six months, a small rotation back toward exposed occupations. Layoffs also remain below normal and are still falling while hiring stays flat, which looks like a frozen hiring market rather than active firing, a materially milder story than the word displacement usually implies. Long-term unemployment ticked up only slightly and broader underemployment actually improved over the year: none of that is the signature of workers being pushed out and landing badly.

Pay adds a caveat rather than an answer: exposed-industry pay is still growing faster than control-industry pay, by 0.81 points in August versus 1.0 in July, but that gap is inflated by employment shrinking in exposed industries and cannot be read as a genuine pay signal on its own.

What to watch: the reabsorption panel's prime-age employment rate is the one that would move first if this is turning into something more serious, since it is the piece that just cracked this month while everything else held.
