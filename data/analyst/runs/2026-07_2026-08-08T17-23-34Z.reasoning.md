*Can anything but my verdict being wrong carry it there?* Recent range of this field, from the joint table's employment_change_12m column: over the last thirteen listed months it runs from -0.5 (2025-07, 2026-06) to +0.3 (2026-03, 2026-05), with a rough standard deviation around 0.25. **-0.5 has been reached twice in that span with nothing else deteriorating and reversed within a month or two each time, so -0.5 discriminates nothing** and I have deliberately not set it there. -0.6 is beyond anything the observation window has produced since the pandemic ended. The Census control re-basing that puts level breaks in both legs each January is the confounder to respect — but it lands in January, outside this 90-day window, and the ratio itself re-bases numerator and denominator together with January steps of 0.2 and 0.1 points, inside its ordinary monthly variation. So a data artefact of that kind cannot reach -0.6 either.

*Can it fire?* Yes. The field moved from +0.3 to -0.5 in a single print between 2026-05 and 2026-06 — an 0.8 swing in one month — so a further 0.6 over three prints is well inside demonstrated behaviour. The 3-month change is already -0.3 and falling at 1.95σ, which is precisely the configuration that would carry the 12-month figure down if it persists. This is a monthly household-survey series with an as_of of 2026-07, so three further observations (August, September, October data) publish before 2026-11-06. This is not a quarterly series that could sleep through its own test window.

Secondary: `exposed_vs_control_jobs.control_value` at or above **0.8 percent year-over-year**. Currently 1.16. This is what buys the discrimination: it requires the control industries — construction, leisure/hospitality, education/health — to still be adding jobs at a healthy clip while the aggregate prime-age employment share falls hard. A general recession would drag control below 0.8 and the compound condition would not fire, which is correct, because a general recession is not what this dashboard is for. Both conditions must hold. Control has run comfortably above 0.8 through the recent record, so this is a live constraint rather than a formality, and it is monthly CES, publishing three more times inside the window.

If that pair fires — prime-age employment share falling at -0.6 or worse while control industries still grow at 0.8%+ — then the outflow from AI-exposed work is no longer landing, and the reallocation reading I have published here is wrong.
