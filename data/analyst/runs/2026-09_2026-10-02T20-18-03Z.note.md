# Published note 2026-09 (run 2026-10-02T20:18:03Z)

Notification: Reading flips to augmentation, though little moved — absence of harm, not proof of gains.
Falsifier: The reading is wrong if the reabsorption panel's prime-age employment-population ratio (25-54 EPOP) 12-month change drops to -0.1 points or below while control-industry job growth (construction, leisure/hospitality, education/health) stays at or above 0.5% YoY — that combination would mean exposed-industry outflows are no longer landing even though the rest of the economy is fine.

---

This month's reading is augmentation, but the more honest description is that displacement hasn't started rather than that augmentation is visibly working: exposed-industry workers don't look stranded, but nothing here shows them being made more valuable by AI either.

What's new is modest. The gap between job growth in AI-exposed industries and in control industries (construction, leisure and hospitality, education and health) narrowed slightly, from about 2.9 points behind to about 2.8 points behind. The pay advantage exposed industries have been showing over control industries also narrowed a touch, from 0.8 points to 0.7 points. Borrowing-cost spreads shifted slightly further from recession-warning territory. None of these three moves is large. The panel the verdict actually rests on — whether people who leave AI-exposed industries land somewhere else, tracked through the share of prime-age adults employed — didn't update this month at all; it's flat over the past year, same as last month. The headline call flipped from displacement last month to augmentation this month while the underlying numbers barely moved, which is worth noting plainly rather than letting the label change do more work than the data did.

The strongest evidence for this reading is that reabsorption panel: the employment share for prime-age adults is flat over the past year and sits above its pre-pandemic level, hiring is running ahead of quitting and pulling further ahead, layoffs are falling, and the share of people stuck in long unemployment spells is shrinking. Three of four signals agreeing in the healthy direction, alongside a flat headline, is the pattern you'd expect if exposed-industry weakness were being absorbed rather than left to fester.

The strongest evidence against it is that the exposed-industry weakness itself isn't new and isn't closing. Job growth in AI-exposed industries has been running well behind control industries for several months running, with no real narrowing. A sharper version of the same signal: postings for knowledge work are still about 22% below their pre-pandemic level, while postings for hands-on work in control industries sit about 8% above theirs — and that gap hasn't moved at all in six months. If this were a healing reallocation story, exposed-industry hiring demand would be expected to show some recovery. It isn't. That absence of improvement is the clearest sign against this reading. A separate caution: the pay gap favoring exposed industries can't currently be trusted as good news, because exposed-industry employment is shrinking by about 1.6% a year, and a shrinking, more senior workforce can show rising average pay without anyone actually getting a raise.

Watch the reabsorption panel, particularly its long-term unemployment reading — the most direct signal of whether people leaving AI-exposed industries are failing to land elsewhere, and the one piece of that panel that moved the wrong way this quarter.
