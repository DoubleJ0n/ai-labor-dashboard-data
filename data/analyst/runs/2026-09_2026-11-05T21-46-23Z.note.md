The reading remains AUGMENTATION: no confirmed sign yet that AI is displacing workers, though this rests mainly on an absence of deterioration rather than clear proof that AI is helping exposed workers. Almost nothing moved this month. Of twelve tracked measures, eleven are unchanged from last time. The only shift is economy-wide productivity growth, which eased slightly from 2.2% to 2.1%, still above its long-run average but now two quarters below boom pace and drifting down. Because this measure covers the whole economy rather than AI-exposed industries specifically, it cannot be pinned to AI either way.

The strongest support for this reading: the prime-age (25 to 54) employment rate has held flat over the past year and sits three-quarters of a point above its pre-pandemic level, while layoffs remain low and falling. If AI were displacing workers broadly, this is one of the first places it should show up as an outright decline, and it has not.

The strongest evidence against it: AI-exposed industries lost 1.6% of jobs over the past year while industries AI barely touches, construction, hospitality, education and health, gained 1.19%, and that gap has now held steady for four straight months rather than closing. Separately, job postings for knowledge work sit 21.6% below their pre-pandemic level while postings for hands-on work sit 8.1% above theirs, a gap that hasn't moved in six months. If this were ordinary reallocation with workers landing elsewhere, some recovery in knowledge-work hiring demand would be expected by now. There is none, and that should not be understated. A plausible non-AI explanation, 2022's interest-rate tightening, accounts for the postings gap's level but not why it refuses to close even as the yield curve has normalized.

Watch the job postings spread, the freshest data on the dashboard: a turn upward in knowledge-work postings would be the first sign this is resolving; continued flatness or further decline would be the first sign it isn't.
