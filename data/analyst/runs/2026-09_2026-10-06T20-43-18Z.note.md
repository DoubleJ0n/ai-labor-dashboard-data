Falsifier: This reading is wrong if the reabsorption panel's prime-age employment ratio (12-month change) falls to -1.0 or worse while control-industry job growth stays at or above 0.5%, showing exposed-industry losses no longer being absorbed in an otherwise healthy economy.

---

The augmentation reading holds this month, and it still rests on an absence of deterioration rather than clear evidence AI is helping these workers. Almost nothing moved since last month: job postings in AI-heavy industries slipped slightly further below their pre-pandemic level while postings elsewhere stayed higher than theirs, but this gap has been flat for six months and mostly traces back to a 2022 hiring pullback that predates current AI use, not a new signal. Every other panel held at its prior reading.

The strongest support for augmentation is that the share of 25-to-54-year-olds who are employed has not budged in 12 months and sits above its pre-pandemic level, while hiring and quitting in the job market stay healthy and layoffs keep falling. That is the opposite of what you would expect if workers pushed out of AI-exposed industries were ending up stuck out of work.

The strongest evidence against it is that employment in AI-exposed industries fell 1.6% over the past year while employment in less-exposed industries rose 1.19%, a gap that has now sat outside its normal historical range for two straight months without narrowing. Layered on top of that, the share of unemployed workers who have been out of work long-term has climbed 3.5 percentage points over the year. It has not yet crossed the level that would flip this call, but it is the one number moving the wrong way while everything else holds flat, and it is exactly what you would see first if displaced workers were quietly leaving the workforce rather than showing up as unemployed, which the flat employment number would not catch.

What to watch: the prime-age employment ratio on the reabsorption panel, alongside the long-term unemployment share next to it. If either turns, this is where it shows first.
